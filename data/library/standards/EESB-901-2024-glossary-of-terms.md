## 1. Purpose

This glossary supports consistent reading of the fictional library. It does not introduce equipment acceptance limits or replace the requirements in the subject standards. A definition helps identify the relevant subject; the numerical requirement should still be traced to the document and clause that state it.

## 2.1. BESS

**BESS** means battery energy storage system. In layout discussions, a BESS container is an individual enclosure forming part of that system. A reference to the storage compound can include several containers and should not be assumed to describe the position or connections of each individual container.

## 2.2. Rapid charger

A **rapid charger** is charging equipment providing the rapid-charging service described in the project equipment schedule. The charger is equipment; a charging bay is the space assigned to a vehicle using that equipment. A bay marking and a charger enclosure are therefore different layout objects.

## 2.3. Design ambient temperature

**Design ambient temperature** is the surrounding temperature adopted for the equipment or cable design assessment. It should be associated with a defined location. It is not automatically an outdoor temperature, a measured commissioning temperature or a battery-cell operating temperature.

## 2.4. Feeder

A **feeder** is a circuit supplying a downstream board or item of equipment from an identified origin. A feeder record should state its destination and function. Similar lengths do not make an AC power feeder and a DC array circuit the same category.

## 2.5. Earth electrode

An **earth electrode** is the part of the earthing arrangement providing the connection with the ground. A measured electrode resistance is a performance result. It is distinct from the count of physical bonds connecting a container to the main earth ring.

## 2.6. Fire barrier

A **fire barrier** is the intervening construction identified as part of a barrier-based separation arrangement. A barrier description should be read with the applicable rating and extent provisions of the current siting document. Calling an ordinary boundary fence a barrier does not establish those properties.

## 3.1. Current

**Current** identifies an edition in force for the relevant library assessment. Current status should be considered together with any applicable amendments. The most recent year appearing in a filename does not, by itself, demonstrate current status.

## 3.2. Superseded

**Superseded** means replaced by another edition. Such a document can explain past decisions but is not the source of a current requirement. Historic accuracy and present authority are different attributes.

## 3.3. Draft

**Draft** identifies a proposal not yet in force. A draft can inform a future-risk discussion but shall not be represented as a current requirement. A consultation closing date is not automatically a commencement date.

## 3.4. Amendment

An **amendment** changes a stated part of an existing edition. Read the amendment with its base document and retain the unchanged provisions. Its presence does not imply that every clause in the base document has been replaced.
