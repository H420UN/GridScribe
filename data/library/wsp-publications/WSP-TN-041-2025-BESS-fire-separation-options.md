# BESS fire-separation options

| Title block | Detail |
|---|---|
| Document number | WSP-TN-041 |
| Edition | 2025, with 2026 status addendum |

| Author role | Energy Storage Design Lead |
| Scope | Comparing the land and barrier implications of the current BESS separation routes |

## 1. Decision to be recorded

A compact compound is not automatically the lowest-cost arrangement once the barrier option is considered. This note sets out the fictional design decision that should be visible in an options review. It does not provide construction details, cost rates or an independent requirement.

## 2. Current separation options

EESB-204:2025, Clause 4.3, requires **6 m between BESS containers, or 3 m where a fire barrier rated for at least 2 hours is installed between them**.

These are distinct routes. A layout with a distance below 6 m cannot rely on the reduced-distance option while omitting the barrier. A project preference for saving land does not create an additional unbarriered route between the two stated distances.

## 3. Land-take comparison

The unbarriered route commits more spacing between containers. Its effect should be understood against the actual compound arrangement rather than by multiplying a dimension without reference to the layout.

The options review should show which equipment moves and how the revised arrangement relates to the site boundary. Sufficient boundary clearance does not cure inadequate internal spacing, and improving internal spacing should not silently change a previously recorded boundary distance.

## 4. Barrier-cost comparison

The barrier route can preserve a more compact arrangement but introduces a barrier package and its associated evidence. The review should identify that package explicitly rather than treating a barrier as a cost-free line on a sketch.

No cost ranking is universal in this note. Compare project land implications with the barrier provision using project information, and distinguish an option under consideration from the selected design.

## 5. Earlier advice

WSP-TN-017:2022 quotes 3 m from EESB-204:2021, Clause 4.3. That standalone historic allowance is outdated. The current standard takes precedence over the earlier technical note.

A historic project can explain why a smaller spacing appears on an inherited drawing. It does not provide authority to repeat the same arrangement in a current design without meeting the current clause.

## 6. 2026 status addendum

EESB-204:2025 A1:2026 was issued on 1 June 2026. For the 3 m option, the barrier must extend at least **0.5 m beyond the container's height and length**. The amendment changes Clause 4.3 only; it does not change the 6 m or 3 m distances or the 10 m boundary requirement in Clause 4.5.

## 7. Review conclusion

Record the selected route, the layout revision and the evidence supporting that route. For a barrier-based conclusion, cite the base clause with A1:2026. This note supports that decision record, but the requirement remains in the current standard and amendment.
