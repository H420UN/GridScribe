## 1. Project context

Marsh Lane was a fictional **20 MW solar project with two BESS containers**, completed in **2024**. This case study records how the project team coordinated its layout and supporting design information. It is a historic example, not a template establishing the requirements for a new site.

The project is separate from Fen Edge Energy Hub. Equipment counts and capacities in this case study should not be carried into another project's inputs.

## 2. Design basis at completion

The Marsh Lane arrangement used **3 m between the two BESS containers**, designed to **EESB-204:2021, Clause 4.3**. That minimum spacing was correct under the fictional edition applicable at the time.

The case study therefore records a valid historic decision. It does not state that 3 m without the current barrier provisions remains acceptable for a later design.

## 3. Layout coordination

The earlier team maintained a small set of consistent container identifiers across the layout and equipment records. That made discussions of route interfaces and equipment movements more straightforward than using changing informal descriptions.

The useful lesson is to preserve traceability between drawings and schedules. A similar-looking compound on another site does not justify copying the original acceptance values.

## 4. Record preparation

The completion summary separated installed equipment descriptions from the standards clauses used for design acceptance. This allowed a later reader to understand both what was built and which edition had governed the historic decision.

Keeping those facts separate becomes important after a standard changes. A record can remain accurate about the past without remaining a suitable current requirement source.

## 5. Current-status warning

EESB-204:2021 was superseded by EESB-204:2025 on **1 March 2025**. The current Clause 4.3 requires 6 m, or 3 m with a fire barrier rated for at least 2 hours. A1:2026 adds the barrier-extent provision.

Do not use the historic Marsh Lane spacing to pass a new layout. Retrieve the current standard and applicable amendment, then compare the new project's actual arrangement against them.

## 6. Reusable lessons

Reuse a clear document structure, a consistent equipment naming approach and the practice of recording edition references. Do not reuse project capacity, container count or separation values as though they were general rules.

When presenting a precedent, label its date and design basis alongside the quoted arrangement. This prevents a reader seeing a number without the context that made it valid.

## 7. Review conclusion

Marsh Lane demonstrates historic compliance with the 2021 edition, not present authority for a 3 m unbarriered layout. A current report may mention this case study to explain an inherited assumption, but its acceptance conclusion should come from the current standard rather than this narrative.
