## 1. Use of the checklist

Use this checklist to organise a review, not as a substitute for the subject standards. A complete-looking table can still contain the wrong edition or a requirement applied to the wrong equipment. The review should examine the reasoning behind each conclusion as well as whether every cell has text.

## 2. Requirement citation

For every requirement, record the **document number, edition and clause**. A document title alone is insufficient where several editions exist. A clause number alone is insufficient where different standards use the same numbering.

Keep the source of the project value separate from the source of the requirement. A cable schedule supplies a calculated result; it does not establish the allowed maximum.

## 3. Status check

Check the status of every cited standard. A document marked **SUPERSEDED** may explain historic decisions but should not provide a current acceptance criterion. Do not select an older edition merely because it produces a more convenient answer.

Where an old technical note conflicts with a current standard, follow the standard and explain the conflict. A supporting note is not a separate authority able to override a current clause.

## 4. Draft handling

Flag relevant drafts as **future risks**, not current requirements. Keep proposed values and consultation dates visibly labelled. A later year in the filename does not establish that a document is in force.

An arrangement can satisfy the current standard and still merit a discussion of future flexibility. The report should communicate both points without turning an advisory concern into a false non-compliance.

## 5. Amendment check

Use the **latest applicable amendment** with its base edition. Check what the amendment actually changes. Do not assume that a short amendment replaces every clause in the original document.

For a barrier-based BESS spacing conclusion, the record should include EESB-204:2025, Clause 4.3, and A1:2026. The added extent provision should not be misreported as a change to the basic separation distances.

## 6. Evidence and uncertainty

Keep measured results, calculated results and design descriptions distinguishable. Where a source does not establish an answer, record the evidence gap rather than inventing a value or a citation.

Do not turn every item outside a short project extract into an additional failure. Describe the scope of the supplied evidence and distinguish it from a demonstrated non-compliance.

## 7. Review and sign-off

Mark generated report text **DRAFT – for review by a chartered electrical engineer**. A chartered engineer signs off the report after checking the project values, source editions and conclusions.

The reviewer should open the cited clause, compare the number and subject, and confirm that the stated action follows from the actual finding. A plausible citation format is not proof that the cited text supports the claim.
