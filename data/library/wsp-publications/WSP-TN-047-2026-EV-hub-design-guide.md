## 1. Review approach

A charging-hub review should distinguish the requirements in force from proposals that may affect a future phase. Treating every newer document as mandatory can create false non-compliances. Ignoring a relevant draft altogether can also miss an opportunity to preserve inexpensive flexibility.

This note supports a balanced fictional review. It does not adopt the draft as a project requirement.

## 2. Current charger separation

EESB-305:2022, Clause 6.4, requires rapid chargers to be at least **10 m from any BESS container**. Use that current clause for the acceptance statement. Identify the nearest charger-to-container relationship so that the design value is understandable.

Do not substitute a BESS-to-boundary distance or a charging-bay width for the actual charger-to-container relationship. Similar-looking dimensions can describe different design questions.

## 3. Emergency stops

EESB-305:2022, Clause 6.6, requires an **emergency stop within sight of every charging bay**. The review should show which stop provides the visible provision for each bay.

The sign identifying the stop location is a separate matter under EESB-320:2025. A sign alone is not evidence that the actual stop is within sight, and a visible stop does not by itself establish the complete sign information.

## 4. Consultation proposal

EESB-305:2026 DRAFT, proposed Clause 6.4, proposes **15 m** between rapid chargers and BESS containers. It is not in force. Consultation closes **31 January 2027**.

Use the words “draft proposal” when discussing that distance. The consultation closing date is not a commencement date, and this note does not predict whether the proposal will be adopted unchanged.

## 5. Future flexibility

Where it is cheap to do so, consider reserving space that could support a later layout change. Describe this as an advisory design choice rather than a correction to a current failure.

If reserving space has a substantial consequence, make that consequence visible in the options discussion. An open-ended instruction to future-proof the site is less useful than a clear record of what flexibility is being preserved and why.

## 6. Example of a clear conclusion

A nearest separation of 12 m exceeds the current 10 m minimum. It would be below the draft's proposed 15 m, so a report can state current compliance and separately flag a future risk. Calling the same arrangement non-compliant solely against the draft would confuse present requirements with a proposal.

## 7. Source hierarchy

Cite EESB-305:2022 for the current requirement and label the draft separately. This technical note is supporting commentary. Bay dimensions and signage in EESB-320:2025 are useful related subjects, but that document does not supply the BESS separation requirement.
